## Finding 1
CISA added two JFrog Artifactory vulnerabilities to its Known Exploited Vulnerabilities catalog, based on evidence of active exploitation: CVE-2026-42016 (CVSS 8.1), an incorrect-authorization flaw that lets a low-privilege token be exchanged for an administrator-scope token, and CVE-2026-42018 (CVSS 7.5), an improper-authentication flaw that returns an internal anonymous-user token to an unauthenticated caller even when anonymous access is disabled. Wiz Research observed multiple threat actors chaining the two flaws between August 15 and September 8, 2026, turning an unauthenticated request into an admin-scoped token in two steps; on compromised servers attackers created persistent administrator accounts (including ones named jfrog-distribution and backup-service), deployed malicious Groovy plugins, and installed Rust-based backdoors with command-and-control capabilities. The same campaign also used a third flaw, CVE-2026-82329 (CVSS 9.8), an unauthenticated authentication bypass in JFrog Access that was exploited September 1-8. The two flaws were first disclosed July 27 (42016) and August 12 (42018); as of mid-September, Wiz measured 59% of organizations still vulnerable to CVE-2026-42016 and 62% to CVE-2026-42018. JFrog Cloud instances were patched automatically; self-hosted Artifactory must be upgraded to the fixed build for its release branch: for CVE-2026-42016, version 7.133.11 (affected: prior to 7.133.11); for CVE-2026-42018, 7.111.20, 7.117.27, 7.125.19, 7.133.28, or 7.146.8 (affected: below those on the 7.111 through 7.146 branches); for CVE-2026-82329, 7.111.21, 7.117.28, 7.125.20, 7.133.29, 7.146.38, or 7.161.20 (affected: 7.111.4 through 7.111.21, and 7.117.0-7.117.27, 7.125.0-7.125.19, 7.133.0-7.133.28, 7.146.0-7.146.36, 7.161.0-7.161.19). Patching does not remove attacker-created admin accounts or rotate stolen cluster join keys, so internet-exposed servers unpatched between August 15 and September 8 must be treated as potentially compromised. Federal agencies must remediate the two JFrog KEV entries by September 25, 2026.

## Finding 2
CISA added CVE-2026-84869 (CVSS 9.9) in ConnectWise ScreenConnect to its Known Exploited Vulnerabilities catalog on September 11, 2026, after confirming active exploitation in the wild. The flaw is a missing authorization and improper privilege management issue in the ScreenConnect client that lets files be transferred and executed through an active remote session without authorization or confirmation from the host. Huntress warned in early September that the vulnerability has been exploited in the wild since August 20; attackers used social engineering to trick victims into running rogue ScreenConnect clients, which then pushed four VBScript files designed for persistence and propagation to other ScreenConnect clients, producing worm-like behavior. The flaw affects ScreenConnect client versions prior to 26.6.5 (the server component is not directly affected; ConnectWise cloud servers were already updated); on-premise deployments must upgrade to 26.6.5 or later, then reinstall host clients and update access agents. As a temporary mitigation, ConnectWise recommends disabling the TransferFiles permission in ScreenConnect. CISA required federal agencies to remediate by September 14, 2026.
