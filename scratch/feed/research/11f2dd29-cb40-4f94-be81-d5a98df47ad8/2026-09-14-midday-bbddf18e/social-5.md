## Finding 1
A Threads post by 王志灃 (@wang_chih_feng), dated 2026-09-08, reports a token-consumption failure in Codex multi-agent workflows. The problem, he argues, is not the subagent itself but the parent agent: while waiting for results it repeatedly calls wait_agent every 30-60 seconds, and each empty poll reprocesses the large parent context, silently inflating input tokens. Commenters corroborated the finding with their own measurements — one reported that after running two luna subagents, just 4 empty polls reprocessed 636k input tokens. The thread attributes the behavior to recent updates and notes that Opus Fable does not appear to have the issue. One commenter shared a GitHub workflow they built that manages 53 agents over more than four hours without wasting orchestrator tokens, as a workaround. Source quality: the author's account is small (130 followers) but the claim carries corroborating measurements from commenters in a technical troubleshooting thread.

## Finding 2
A Threads post by Ali Esk (@alieskand.on), dated 2026-09-13, describes a fleet-observability workflow: Dosu runs 54 production agents across more than 697,000 runs, and its engineers query production behavior from inside the coding agent using Pydantic Logfire MCP instead of manually reviewing long traces. Commenters expanded on the reported outcomes, including debugging time cut by roughly 90%, faster root-cause analysis, discovery of hidden cost bugs, and annual savings. One commenter cautioned that the coding agent's production access should stay read-only so a misdiagnosis cannot cause an incident. The discussion frames the trend as production telemetry becoming an active tool for improving agents rather than just dashboards.
- https://www.threads.com/@alieskand.on/post/DdOkNi0E4yn (published 2026-09-13)

## Finding 3
An Instagram reel posted 2026-09-14 by creator Owly (@t_explains_it_all) presents a local-inference benchmark: a 176-billion-parameter model, Qwen3 8 Flash Next, running on a modest 12GB RTX 4070 Super with 48GB of system RAM, with 51 billion parameters parked in a lookup table on an NVMe drive and read as needed. After tuning context settings and enabling MTP, the reported performance is 28-32 tokens per second at 32,000-token prompts, dropping to about 20 tokens per second at 100,000-token prompts. The presenter states he uses IQ3_XXS compression, so full-model benchmark scores are not guaranteed, but says the setup has been more than enough for his coding, debugging, and agent workflows. He teases a follow-up video with the exact setup and settings.
- https://www.instagram.com/reel/DdQcg8XRt0x/ (published 2026-09-14)

## Finding 4
A Facebook group post by AI systems architect Marc Lavelle (@themarclavelle, 1.8K followers), posted 2026-09-12, shows a monitor screenshot of a 42-minute-24-second multi-agent workflow: 39 agents burning 2.2 million tokens, all on the Fable 5.1 model, on the task 'Verify, adversarially attack, and gap-check the 9/12 Grok annuity-tool memo; synthesize reconciliation v2.' Two phases are visible: Verify at 10/12 complete and Refute at 7/24 complete. His caption: 'If you're not using your AI tools like this, are you really using them at all?' The thread evolved into practical discussion of a repo-as-memory pattern — append-only logs, decision records, and START HERE files — with one commenter claiming their own record of 456 agents and roughly 15 million tokens.
