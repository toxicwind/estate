# Provenance Brief: 差序格局, 关系, Fei Xiaotong (费孝通), 《乡土中国》

Research date: 2026-10-01. All citations below were read directly; URLs are verbatim from search/fetch results. No citations invented.

---

## 1. 差序格局 (chàxù géjú) — the concept and its original context

**Coiner:** Fei Xiaotong (费孝通) in 《乡土中国》 (*Xiangtu Zhongguo*), chapter 4, titled "差序格局".

**Original serial context.** The book is a compilation of 14 essays Fei wrote for the journal 《世纪评论》 (*Shiji Pinglun* / Century Review), based on his "乡土社会学" (rural sociology) lecture notes from courses taught at 西南联大 (Southwest Associated University) and 云南大学 (Yunnan University) in the late 1940s. (Source: Chinese edition publisher's description, People's Publishing House editions, via https://pdf.onlinetoolsland.com/3283983.pdf: "这里收集的是我在40年代后期，根据作者在西南联大和云南大学所讲'乡村社会学'一课的内容，应当时《世纪评论》之约，而写成分期连载的14篇文章。" / "Collected here are the 14 essays written in installments at the invitation of *Shiji Pinglun*, based on the 'rural sociology' course I taught at Southwest Associated University and Yunnan University in the late 1940s.")

**Full chapter sequence** (14 essays + postscript), confirming 差序格局 as chapter 4: 乡土本色 / 文字下乡 / 再论文字下乡 / 差序格局 / 系维着私人的道德 / 家族 / 男女有别 / 礼治秩序 / 无讼 / 无为政治 / 长老统治 / 血缘和地缘 / 名实的分离 / 从欲望到需要 / 后记. (Same source.)

**Original Chinese formulation** (as quoted in secondary discussion citing Fei, via https://max.book118.com/try_down/718044142141006131.pdf, a 高考语文 study document quoting 李鹏亚《读乡土中国——对差序格局的理解》):

> "中国的社会结构是像一块石头丢在水面上所发生的一圈圈推出去的波纹。'每个人都是他社会影响所推出去的圈子的中心。被圈子的波纹所推及的就发生联系。每个人在某一时间某一地点所动用的圈子是不一定相同的'。'这个网络像个蜘蛛的网，有一个中心，就是自己。'"
>
> "在差序格局中，社会关系是逐渐从一个一个人推出去的，是私人联系的增加，社会范围是一根根私人联系所构成的网络，因之，我们传统社会里所有的社会道德也只在私人联系中发生意义。"

**Hamilton/Zheng English rendering** ("differential mode of association"), via JSTOR chapter pages for *From the Soil* (https://www.jstor.org/stable/10.1525/j.ctt1pn6km — search-result snippets; direct page fetch was blocked by a JSTOR client challenge, noted as failed access):

> "The basic structure of Chinese rural society is what I have called 'a differential mode of association' (*chaxugeju*). This pattern is composed of distinctive networks spreading out from each individual's personal connections. It is quite different from the modern Western organizational mode of association (*tuantigeju*)."

And from the following chapter (on patrilineages):

> "I proposed several concepts — in particular, 'differential mode of association' (*chaxugeju*) and 'organizational mode of association' (*tuantigeju*). I knew that these unfamiliar terms would cause troubles for my readers, but I had to create them in order to conceptualize clearly those contexts that are currently absent from sociological terminology."

**Fei's own gloss on the coinage** (same JSTOR source): he states he "had to create" these terms because the contexts were "currently absent from sociological terminology" — i.e., 差序格局 was a deliberate neologism, not a pre-existing folk term.

**Western contrast term:** 团体格局 (*tuantigeju*, "organizational mode of association") — Fei's ripples-vs-rice-straw-bundles contrast: "西洋社会是团体社会…像由几根草束成一把，几把束成一扎" (Western society is an organizational society, "like straw bound into bundles"). (Same book118 source.)

## 2. 关系 (guānxì) — the term's provenance

**Critical distinction:** 关系 as an *analyzed academic concept* is younger than 差序格局 and does not originate with Fei as a term of art, though Fei's framework is its standard theoretical ancestor.

- A UTS ePress thesis on guanxi (https://opus.lib.uts.edu.au/bitstream/10453/11445/1/2008000779.pdf) states: "Given the word 'guanxi' was not found in the old Chinese dictionary, some scholars argue that the concept of guanxi was a 'new thing' emerging in the late Cultural Revolution (1966–1976) and prospering in the economic reform period (Langenberg, 2007; Wilson, 2002; Yang, 1994)."
- The same source: "Yang (1994) states that the essence of guanxi reflects the important rules of Confucian relational morality — the five key relationships" (五伦 *wu lun*: emperor–subject, father–son, husband–wife, elder–younger brother, friend–friend).
- Foundational academic works routinely cited as a triple: "Fei (1992 [1947]), Yang (1994), and Gold et al. (2002)" — via ResearchGate page for *Gifts, Favors, and Banquets* (https://www.researchgate.net/publication/329252356_Gifts_Favors_and_Banquets_The_Art_of_Social_Relationships_in_China).
- **Mayfair Mei-hui Yang**, *Gifts, Favors, and Banquets: The Art of Social Relationships in China* (Cornell University Press, 1994; ISBN 9780801495922, 384 pp.) — the landmark ethnography that made guanxi a formal academic object. Yang: professor, UC Santa Barbara (Religious Studies / East Asian Languages and Cultural Studies); PhD UC Berkeley. (Bio: http://www.socsc.hku.hk/ccspl/myang/pdf/Biography.pdf; edition data via https://www.abebooks.co.uk/9780801495922/Gifts-Favors-Banquets-Art-Social-080149592X/plp.) Publisher's synopsis: "An elaborate and pervasive set of practices, called guanxi, underlies everyday social relationships in contemporary China."
- A Roskilde University thesis (https://rucforsk.ruc.dk/ws/files/124895945/Guanxi_Final_Project.pdf) explicitly roots guanxi analysis in Fei: "one can return to the seminal work of classical Chinese sociologist 费孝通 (Fei Xiaotong). In his 1948 magnum opus, *From the Soil*… Fei articulated the theory of 差序格局 (Chaxugeju)," then quotes the rice-straw-bundles passage (pp. 61–63 of the translation).

**Fei's own adjacent vocabulary:** In *From the Soil*, the operative terms are 差序格局, 团体格局, and 私 (the "selfish"/private problem); *guanxi* as such is discussed by Wikipedia's summary of the book ("he explains how kinship form the nexus of social relationships, governed under rules called *guanxi*") but the formal guanxi literature postdates Fei by decades. (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/From_the_Soil, Themes section.)

## 3. Fei Xiaotong (费孝通, 1910–2005) — biographical anchor points

- Born 1910, Wujiang County, Jiangsu; died April 24, 2005, Beijing, age 94. (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fei_Xiaotong; NYT obituary citation: Saxon, Wolfgang, May 9, 2005, "Fei Xiaotong, 94, a Pioneer in Chinese Anthropology, Is Dead," *The New York Times* — cited via Wikipedia; the NYT page itself was not directly fetched.)
- Studied sociology under Wu Wenzao at Yanjing University (1933); anthropology under Russian ethnologist S. M. Shirokogoroff at Tsinghua (1935). (CLACSO repository bio: https://biblioteca-repositorio.clacso.edu.ar/bitstream/CLACSO/8804/1/pdf_813.pdf)
- 1936–1938: London School of Economics under Bronisław Malinowski; PhD 1938 based on Kaixiangong (開弦弓) village fieldwork; published as *Peasant Life in China* (Routledge/Dutton, 1939). Malinowski's preface called it "a landmark in the development of anthropological fieldwork and theory." (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fei_Xiaotong; https://www.berose.fr/article1625.html)
- First fieldwork in Guangxi ended in tragedy: his leg crushed by a tiger trap; his pregnant bride and co-worker Wang Tonghui (王同惠) drowned seeking help. (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fei_Xiaotong)
- Awards: Malinowski Prize (Society for Applied Anthropology, 1980); Huxley Memorial Medal (Royal Anthropological Institute, 1981); Encyclopaedia Britannica Prize (1988); Ramon Magsaysay Award for Community Leadership (1994). (Same sources.)
- Political arc: branded "rightist"/"anti-Marxist" after 1949 despite early sympathy for the CCP; works banned on the mainland and restricted in Taiwan; available in Chinese only in Hong Kong until his rehabilitation in 1979 and appointment at Peking University. (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/From_the_Soil)
- China Daily biographical piece: "Fei Xiaotong's life: China's true story," April 27, 2005. (Cited via Wikipedia references.)

## 4. 《乡土中国》 publication provenance — the 1947/1948 discrepancy

Two dates circulate; both are correct for different events:

| Event | Date | Source |
|---|---|---|
| 14 essays serialized in 《世纪评论》 | 1947 | Wikipedia (*From the Soil*): "first published in 1947"; UC Press copy: "first published in 1947" |
| Book edition, Shanghai: 观察社 (Guancha / The Observer) | 1948 | en-academic bibliography: "*Xiangtu Zhongguo* 鄉土中國 (Rural China). Shanghai: Guancha, 1948"; Wikipedia (Fei Xiaotong): "Shanghai: Guancha, 1948"; People's Daily 副刊 (Zhao Xudong, 2025-10-31): "《乡土中国》一书，最初由上海观察社1948年出版" |
| Companion volume 《乡土重建》 (*Xiangtu chongjian*), Shanghai: Guancha | 1948 | Same bibliography |
| English translation, UC Press | 1992 | Below |

The People's Daily piece (http://paper--people--com--cn--0907hml8ac19b.npsv6.com/rmrb/pc/attachement/202510/31/e721ad81-9566-41c1-b250-4e3f6601c3fa.pdf, Zhao Xudong: "《乡土中国》…就是20世纪40年代费孝通在西南联大开设'乡村社会学'课程的副产品，他陆陆续续在《世纪评论》上发表了14篇文章") confirms the lecture-course → journal-serialization → book pipeline and dates the book to 观察社 1948.

**Confusion warning (flagged in scholarship):** the title *Earthbound China* properly belongs to a different book — Fei & Zhang Zhiyi (Chang Chih-I), *Earthbound China: A Study of Rural Economy in Yunnan* (University of Chicago Press, 1945; London edition 1945). A JCGCP journal article (https://globalcenturypress.com/wp-content/uploads/2024/01/JCGCP-EN-2016-10.pdf, pp. 107–113) notes the author "liked the translation of the title as 'From the soil', because the translation as 'Earthbound China' was always leading to confusion with Fei Hsiao-Tung and Chang Chih-I's *Earthbound China: A Study of Rural Economy in Yunnan* (London, 1945)."

**English translation:** *From the Soil: The Foundations of Chinese Society*, trans. Gary G. Hamilton and Wang Zheng, University of California Press, Berkeley, August 28, 1992; 176 pp.; ISBN 9780520077966 (ISBN-10: 0520077962). Translators' introduction covers Fei's education, the fate of his writings on/off the mainland, and the analysis's sociological significance; epilogue covers the reform program of the companion text *Xiangtu chongjian*. (Publisher copy via https://www.readings.com.au/product/9780520077966/9780520077966 and https://tertulia.com/book/from-the-soil-the-foundations-of-chinese-society-xiaotong-fei/9780520077966.) JSTOR e-book: https://www.jstor.org/stable/10.1525/j.ctt1pn6km (eISBN 978-0-520-91248-9; institutional access required — direct fetch blocked).

**Reception datum:** "according to *The New York Times*, [*From the Soil*] is a 'cornerstone of modern sociology and anthropology.'" (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/From_the_Soil, citing NYT via Saxon obituary.)
