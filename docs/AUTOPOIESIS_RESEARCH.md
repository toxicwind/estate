## Core Invariant

An autopoietic system is a network of production processes that satisfies two conditions:

1. **Self-regeneration**: The network continually regenerates and realizes the very production relations that produced its components. Every component is produced by the network, and every element of the network requires components to exist. This is a circular, self-referential closure.

2. **Self-constitution as a concrete unity**: The system constitutes itself as a concrete unity within a boundary-defined space — and that boundary is itself **produced by the system's own internal processes** (e.g., membrane lipids synthesized by the cell's own metabolism).

**The invariant is: the organization (the closed network of production relations) persists through the continual destruction and replacement of material components (structure). Structure changes; organization is conserved. The boundary is load-bearing — actively synthesized by the system's metabolic processes and constitutive of the system's spatial identity.**

> "An autopoietic machine is organized as a network of production processes that (i) produce components which continuously regenerate the network of processes that produced them, and (ii) constitute the machine as a concrete unity in a boundary-defined space."
> — Varela, *Principles of Biological Autonomy* (1979)

## 8 Papers/Domains with Extracted Invariants

### 1. Maturana & Varela (1973/1980) — *Autopoiesis and Cognition*

- **Full citation**: Maturana, H.R. & Varela, F.J. (1980). *Autopoiesis and Cognition: The Realization of the Living*. Dordrecht: D. Reidel; Springer Boston Studies in the Philosophy and History of Science, vol. 42. DOI: [10.1007/978-94-009-8947-4](https://doi.org/10.1007/978-94-009-8947-4). Originally published in Spanish as *De Maquinas y Seres Vivos* (1972).
- **Domain**: Foundational autopoietic theory / biology of cognition
- **Invariant**: Living systems are closed, self-referential networks of molecular productions that continuously regenerate the very network that produced them. The system constitutes its own boundary (membrane) as a product of its internal metabolic processes. "Living systems conserve organization" — their internal autopoietic organization endures while material structures change. Cognition is intrinsic to this circular organization.
- **Key concepts**: Organization vs. structure; circular production; self-produced boundary; cognition as biological phenomenon; structural coupling.

### 2. Maturana (1970) — *Biology of Cognition*

- **Full citation**: Maturana, H.R. (1970). *Biology of Cognition*. Biological Computer Laboratory Research Report BCL 9.0. University of Illinois Urbana-Champaign. Reprinted in *Autopoiesis and Cognition* (1980), pp. 5–58.
- **Domain**: Biology of cognition / enactive approach
- **Invariant**: A living system is a unit of interactions defined by a closed circular organization. The circular organization determines the components that specify the system; these components are defined by their role in preserving the circularity. The system remains a unit of interactions only so long as this circular organization is unbroken. Living systems are cognitive domains — their identity is maintained relative to an observer, and their functional significance is tied to maintaining circularity.
- **Key concepts**: Circular organization as invariant; unit of interactions; niche as defined by organization; living system as inferential/cognitive domain.

### 3. Varela (1979) — *Principles of Biological Autonomy*

- **Full citation**: Varela, F.J., Maturana, H.R., & Uribe, R. (1979). *Principles of Biological Autonomy*. New York: Plenum Press. New edition edited by E. Di Paolo & E. Thompson (2015). Cambridge, MA: MIT Press. DOI: [10.7551/mitpress/14875](https://doi.org/10.7551/mitpress/14875).
- **Domain**: Biological autonomy / enactive cognition
- **Invariant**: Autonomy in living systems arises from organizational closure — the network of processes that mutually enable and sustain the system as a unified whole within its domain. The invariant organization persists through continuous material exchanges with the environment, enabling self-maintenance and goal-directed behavior without centralized control. The boundary (external membrane) is a produced component of the autopoietic machine, not a pre-given container.
- **Key concepts**: Organizational closure; autonomy; boundary as produced element; six-point criteria for autopoietic machines; embodiment.

### 4. Barandiaran, Di Paolo & Rohde (2012) — *Autopoiesis 40 Years Later*

- **Full citation**: Barandiaran, E., Di Paolo, E., & Rohde, M. (2012). Autopoiesis 40 Years Later. A Review and a Reformulation. *Origins of Life and Evolution of Biospheres*, 42(4), 367–379. DOI: [10.1007/s11084-012-9297-y](https://doi.org/10.1007/s11084-012-9297-y). PMID: 23054553.
- **Domain**: Critical reformulation of autopoiesis
- **Invariant**: Organizational invariance — the stable set of production relations among components remains constant even as individual components change through continual chemical activity. Autopoiesis is defined by resistance to disintegration of its organization, not merely opposition to external perturbations. A molecular autopoietic system serves as a robust demarcation criterion for living beings. Autopoiesis entails closed organizational boundaries, homeostasis, self-reproduction, causally circular processes, and multicellularity as derivative phenomena.
- **Key concepts**: Organizational invariance; molecular autopoietic system; demarcation criterion; resistance to disintegration; reformulation resolving ambiguities.

### 5. Rosen (1991) — *Life Itself*

- **Full citation**: Rosen, R. (1991). *Life Itself: A Comprehensive Inquiry Into the Nature, Origin, and Fabrication of Life*. New York: Columbia University Press.
- **Domain**: Relational biology / closure theory
- **Invariant**: A living organism is **closed to efficient causation** — every efficient cause within the system is produced by the system itself. Formalized as an (M,R)-system (metabolism-repair system): a material system whose relational diagram contains a closed cycle of causal entailments (an impredicative loop). This closure is the invariant that distinguishes a living system from any complex system. Biological explanation requires relational, model-based accounts capturing organismic organization beyond linear causation.
- **Key concepts**: Closure to efficient causation; (M,R)-systems; impredicative causal structure; nonsimulability (not Turing-simulable); relational biology; organism as minimal (M,R)-system.

### 6. Barandiaran (2003) — *What is life for science?*

- **Full citation**: Barandiaran, E. (2003). What is life for science? *Proceedings of Science*, POS(255), 077. DOI: [pos.sissa.it/255/077](https://pos.sissa.it/255/077).
- **Domain**: Philosophy of biology / autopoietic definition of life
- **Invariant**: Life is best described by the invariant network of organizational relations (the autopoietic organization) rather than by variable material components. The autopoietic process — self-generation and self-maintenance of the cell's organization from within its boundary, enabled by continual regeneration of components consumed by metabolism — is the distinguishing feature. Life = autopoietic unity + environment + cognition, where cognition is the organism's sensorimotor capacity to recognize and interact with its environment as defined by its organization.
- **Key concepts**: Invariant organizational relations; autopoietic process as life criterion; cell as autopoietic unit; cognition as sensorimotor interaction; life distinguished from non-life by organizational closure.

### 7. Bich & Arnellos (2012) — *Autopoiesis, Autonomy, and Organizational Biology*

- **Full citation**: Bich, L. & Arnellos, A. (2012). Autopoiesis, Autonomy, and Organizational Biology: Critical Remarks on "Life After Ashby". *Cybernetics and Human Knowing*, 19(4), 75–103. DOI: [10.1007/s10618-012-0137-1](https://doi.org/10.1007/s10618-012-0137-1).
- **Domain**: Organizational biology / critical analysis of autopoiesis
- **Invariant**: Autopoiesis entails self-production and **intrinsic instability**, realizable only in domains with transformative, processual properties like the molecular domain. The invariant is the self-producing organization in the molecular domain, which does not require a sharp organization-structure dualism or reflexive downward causation. Autopoietic organization is inherently unstable and processual — stability arises from continuous production, not from a fixed structure.
- **Key concepts**: Self-production in molecular domain; intrinsic instability; processual organization; critique of Ashby framework; no organization-structure dualism required; autopoiesis vs. homeostasis.

### 8. Froese & Stewart (2010) — *Life After Ashby*

- **Full citation**: Froese, T. & Stewart, J. (2010). Life After Ashby: Ultrastability and the Autopoietic Foundations of Biological Autonomy. *Cybernetics and Human Knowing*, 17(4), 71–89.
- **Domain**: Cybernetics / ultrastability / autopoietic grounding
- **Invariant**: Autopoiesis gains explanatory traction when grounded in **ultrastability** (Ashby) and **far-from-equilibrium chemical self-production**. The invariant is organizational robustness maintained through far-from-equilibrium dynamics — the system maintains its organization not merely through structural closure but through continuous energy throughput that stabilizes the production network against perturbation. Autopoiesis is dynamic and context-dependent; its precise definition reflects current biological understanding.
- **Key concepts**: Ultrastability; far-from-equilibrium; organizational robustness; chemical self-production; grounding autopoiesis in concrete physics; critique of abstract autopoiesis.

## Synthesis: The Load-Bearing Boundary Invariant

The **load-bearing boundary** concept emerges across all 8 papers/domains as a specific refinement of the autopoietic invariant:

| Aspect | Description |
|--------|-------------|
| **What** | The boundary is not externally imposed but is produced by the system's internal processes |
| **Why load-bearing** | It actively defines the system's spatial domain, separates interior from exterior, and is constitutive of the system's identity |
| **How produced** | Through the same metabolic/production network that generates other components (e.g., membrane lipids synthesized by the cell's own metabolism) |
| **Invariant property** | The boundary persists as long as the organizational closure persists, regardless of material turnover |
| **Failure mode** | Breakdown of boundary production = loss of autopoietic identity = system ceases to be a living system |

**The invariant can be stated as**: *A living system exists as long as its autopoietic organization — a closed network of production relations that continually regenerates itself and its own load-bearing boundary — is conserved. The organization is invariant through continual material change (structure). The boundary is load-bearing: it is produced by the network, defines the system's spatial unity, and is constitutive of the system's identity.*

## Application to Sovereign Project Revision

The autopoietic framework provides a theoretical basis for organizational revision principles:

1. **Organizational closure**: The sovereign project's revision constitution should define an organizational closure where all components (services, configurations, processes) are produced by and regenerate the same network of relations.

2. **Load-bearing boundaries**: Project boundaries (service definitions, interfaces, contracts) should be produced by the organization itself, not externally imposed, and should be constitutive of the project's identity.

3. **Invariant through change**: The revision constitution should specify that organizational relations (interfaces, contracts, protocols) are invariant while material implementations (code, configuration, deployment) can change.

4. **Processual stability**: Stability arises from continuous production and maintenance of organizational relations, not from fixed structures — the REORG-PLAN.md already reflects this by using compat symlinks and rename(2) moves rather than flag-day rewrites.
